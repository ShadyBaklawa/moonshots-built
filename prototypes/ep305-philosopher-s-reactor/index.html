An old story: when "precious" metals get cheap

Aluminum was once so hard to refine that it was treated as a precious metal on par with silver — a status symbol rather than something you'd find in a kitchen drawer.

Then a cheap electrolytic refining process arrived in the late 1800s, production scaled, and aluminum went from precious curiosity to the stuff of soda cans within a few decades. If mercury-to-gold transmutation ever became cheap and scalable, gold could in principle face a similar derating — that's the "gold bug" worry the meter above is gesturing at.
