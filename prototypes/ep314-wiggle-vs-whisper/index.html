Whisper vs Wiggle

Two identical tiny neural nets learn the same 2D pattern. One learns by backprop — a single precise whisper of error sent backward. The other learns by weight perturbation — wiggling one weight at a time, checking if it helped.

Simulated teaching toy: both networks, the dataset and the training loops run live in your browser — nothing is pre-recorded. Real-world perturbation research (e.g. intermediate targets, clever sampling) can close the gap shown here faster than this simple nudge-and-check version.

Why this matters at scale: Dave Blundin, on the podcast at 01:19:30, described backprop on a real frontier model as propagating error back through "2 trillion weights" in one shot (as stated on the podcast). This toy uses a handful of weights, but the shape of the cost gap — one pass for backprop, one pass per weight for naive perturbation — is the same idea at a tiny scale.
