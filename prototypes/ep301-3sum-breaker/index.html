What is 3SUM, in plain language?

Hand an algorithm a big list of numbers and ask one question: do any three of them add up to zero? That's 3SUM. It sounds small, but it's a load-bearing puzzle in computer science — tons of other problems can be reshaped into a 3SUM question, so how fast you can solve 3SUM puts a ceiling on how fast you can solve all of them.

Why a 0.005 exponent change is a big deal

For decades, the fastest known general approach needed roughly n² operations, and plenty of researchers suspected that was as good as it could get. As discussed on the podcast, Imad Mustak described an Anthropic model helping mathematicians find a way to push that down to roughly n1.995 — the kind of exponent shave that was thought impossible to find. The relative speedup from that alone is modest, often well under 2×. The point isn't that it makes today's computers blaze through 3SUM instantly — it's that nobody had found any way past the n² wall in a very long time, and this cracked it open.
